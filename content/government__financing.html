Sales Franchise Tax Debt Service Coverage

Municipalities have several bond options to consider when funding building projects. These are three of the bonds Vineyard has considered:
  1. General Obligation (GO) Bonds – These bonds are backed by the full faith and credit of the city, meaning they are repaid through property taxes. Because they rely on taxpayer backing, they typically require voter approval and a property tax rate increase.
  2. Sales Tax Revenue Bonds – These bonds are repaid using revenue generated from city sales tax collections rather than property taxes. They do not require voter approval and are often a preferred choice for cities with strong commercial activity.
  3. Lease Revenue Bonds – These bonds are backed by lease payments from tenants occupying a facility. They are commonly used when a portion of a project is leased to private or public entities. This type of bond typically has a higher interest rate than GO or Sales Tax Revenue bonds. 

Choosing a Funding Approach

For the Vineyard Center, the city has opted for a Sales Tax Revenue Bond for several key reasons:

  • No tax increase – The bond will be repaid through the city’s existing and incoming sales tax revenue, meaning residents will not see an increase in their taxes.
  • Fixed interest rate – The bond’s interest rate will be locked in, protecting the city from market fluctuations.
  • Strong financial position – Vineyard has an excellent rating for this type of bond, thanks to the robust commercial growth across the city and in the new downtown area.

Looking at Cost Distribution and Sustainability

The financing of the Vineyard Center is structured as follows:

  • The Utah City Developer will donate the land on which the Vineyard Center will be built. 
  • Vineyard City will cover two-thirds of the total project cost.
  • Mountainland Association of Governments (MAG) will contribute approximately one-third of the cost.
  • A business incubator will lease space on the fourth floor creating a central hub for economic development in the city. Rent and lease revenue will support Vineyard’s portion of the bond repayment, ensuring a financially sustainable approach.

By leveraging a strategic mix of funding sources, Vineyard is making a smart investment in its future—enhancing services, supporting economic growth, and ensuring financial responsibility. The Vineyard Center will not only serve as a hub for government operations but also as a catalyst for further development and community engagement in our vibrant city.
