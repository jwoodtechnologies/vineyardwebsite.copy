The Vineyard Redevelopment Agency (RDA) is a Community Reinvestment Agency for Vineyard City. Authorized by the State of Utah, reinvestment agencies are formed to oversee redevelopment and revitalization in communities for targeted “project areas.” Utah has over 90 such agencies in cities across the state. These agencies are used to help cities grow their economy, build public projects, fix up old or damaged areas, and improve homes, businesses, and shopping areas. To do this, cities must have an agency like the RDA to manage these resources.

FORMATION
The Vineyard RDA was formed in 2009 to oversee redevelopment of the former Geneva Steel Mill–a former industrial site that shut down and went bankrupt. This left behind a blighted “brownfield” site with significant demolition and environmental clean-up needs. It has taken many years of work to prepare the area for different future land development uses. The Geneva Urban Renewal Project Area is 2,055 acres, or nearly 70% of the entire city. It stretches from the northernmost boundary of the city south to Center St. and from the lake on the west to Geneva Road on the east.

OVERSIGHT
The RDA is overseen by a board made up of the Vineyard City Council who makes decisions related to the designated “project area.” The RDA follows state laws that govern Community Reinvestment Agencies and govern the process for various tax revenue rules related to redevelopment.

PURPOSE
The purpose of the reinvestment agency is to eliminate industrial blight by rehabilitating the area into a vibrant and economically beneficial part of the community. This is done by encouraging and facilitating redevelopment of the site including:

  • Remediating the land through environmental cleanup and demolition work
  • Investing in new public infrastructure including transportation, public utilities, and community open space
  • Facilitating commercial and housing construction
  • Attracting economic development and jobs to the area

The Vineyard RDA follows the “project plan” first adopted in 2011 to guide the selection of projects to fund or incentivize the construction of housing, mixed use developments, commercial development, local infrastructure, and other economic development opportunities. Under state tax laws, the RDA is able to direct a portion of property tax revenues called “tax increment financing” to pay for some redevelopment costs for a period of time. Each phase of the project area can use tax increment revenues for 25 years.

DEVELOPMENT
