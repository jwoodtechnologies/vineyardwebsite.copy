Environmental Remediation
Anderson Geneva is the permitted party responsible for environmental cleanup. Under federal environmental laws including the Resource Conservation and Recovery Act (RCRA), the EPA works with state environmental regulators (Utah Department of Environmental Quality, DEQ) to regulate, permit, and oversee environmental remediation and cleanup (“corrective action”) for sites like former steel mills that contain hazardous solid waste. In the case of the former Geneva Steel mill site, a Utah Hazardous Waste Post-Closure Permit was issued to United States Steel Corporation (USS) and the purchaser of the property Anderson Geneva, LLC for post-closure care and facility-wide corrective action for dealing with the waste and debris leftover after closure of the steel mill. This is a highly regulated process which requires complex planning and approvals by the state and federal government and ongoing testing, reporting, tracking, and compliance for a myriad of clean-up activities. This permit was issued in 2004 and updated in 2010. The former mill site has been issued an EPA ID (EPA RCRA ID: UTD009086133) and cleanup progress is closely monitored to comply with all state and federal environmental laws. Cleanup at the site is still ongoing under the permit issued to Anderson Geneva who regularly reports required information to the Utah DEQ.

One feature of corrective action was construction of a consolidated (or corrected action) area management unit (CAMU):

  • Moving impacted soil into a membrane-covered structure.
  • Overall Work Area (Eastside) - Approximately 400 acres
  • Site Preparation - Approximately 1.2 million cubic yards
  • CAMU Embankment - Approximately 550,000 cubic yards
  • CAMU Area - 33 acres
  • CAMU Height - 46 Feet
  • CAMU Storage Capacity - Approximately 800,000 cubic yards
  • Liner Materials - Approximately 4 Million square feet
  • Final Cover / Cap Materials - Approximately 100,000 cubic yards
  • Interim Grading - Approximately 300,000 cubic yards

Property Values
The intended purpose of RDA’s and the use of tax increment financing is to encourage redevelopment of blighted areas so that the land and property is more economically viable to attract investment and new uses including new businesses and residents. This causes the property to become more valuable which in turn increases property tax revenues for the participating tax entities. The success of the RDA can be measured in the magnitude and speed of the increase in taxable land values.

Since the plan’s adoption in 2011, the increase in property values has been very strong and has even outpaced original estimates in the planned forecast by 45%. This is a significant growth in value over the base value of the blighted land before the project began.
Taxable Value for Taxing Entities
The Plan aims to increase taxable values for participating taxing entities to increase the total property tax revenues as new properties are developed. This chart shows the increase in total taxable value broken down by the share that taxing entities would benefit from for their 25% share of tax increment financing as compared to the base value. Taxing entities have benefitted greatly from the rapid development yielding more tax revenue than if the site had not been developed.
School Tax Revenues
Schools keep 100% of the tax dollars they were already getting before the RDA began. This is the tax revenue from the “base” values of the property as set in 2006. 75% of the new growth dollars are then temporarily reinvested to fund infrastructure in the redevelopment project area. At the end of the tax increment term, schools will permanently receive much more revenue due to increased property values.

This chart shows the school’s 25% share of incremental tax dollars on top of the base tax revenues from the original undeveloped land values. The revenue for schools has grown significantly thanks to development.
Taxable Values Compared to Alternative Scenarios
What if Vineyard never created an RDA for the former Geneva site? Property values have changed in the redevelopment project area compared to raw land values in the surrounding community and other nearby housing developments.

This chart shows a comparison of the Geneva “brownfield” site to a nearby parcel of 40 acres of undeveloped land in American Fork that is currently being leased for agriculture use and taxed under the agricultural “green belt” exemption. Redevelopment has increased the tax base faster than the rate of value growth in land alone. The tax revenue for developed areas far exceeds the tax revenue from greenbelt land.

The following chart shows the taxable value of land in Vineyard’s RDA project area (Phases 3 & 4) compared to:

  • A nearby residential area not in the RDA area (The Homesteads)
  • Agricultural land in residential and commercial zones in nearby American Fork that has remained undeveloped
  • A scenario where the Geneva site had never been redeveloped

The difference is clear: the area in the RDA increased the tax base more than non-RDA areas, —making land more valuable, productive, and beneficial for the entire region.

Job Creation
Increase Jobs With a Wide Range of Skills
The Project Plan calls for creating a large number of jobs with a wide range of skill levels.
Huntsman Cancer Institute
In 2025, the Huntsman Cancer Institute broke ground on a new 272,000 square foot hospital to be located in the new Utah City development in downtown Vineyard development. Huntsman Cancer Institute’s Comprehensive Cancer Center will open to patients in fall 2028.
National Impact
National Job Creators
The Plan aims to attract national tenants and other job creators looking to build in the Mountain West. A number of significant companies with regional significance have already located or announced locations in the redevelopment site including the new Huntsman Cancer Institute.
Transportation
Alternative Transportation Hubs
The Plan seeks to make possible alternative transportation hubs to minimize congestion and improve air quality:

“The Geneva Urban Renewal Area will… lay the framework for a more pedestrian and transit-oriented city… and facilitate the redevelopment of the surrounding area”...“There are currently no public infrastructure improvements within the majority of the Project Area. The construction of the public infrastructure improvements... will support the development contemplated by this Plan” and “Take advantage of the proximity to Utah Lake and the planned future transit station… and create an intermodal hub transit area and offer transportation corridors.”

Work has progressed to successfully bring a commuter rail station to Vineyard and plans are ongoing to add bus and light rail service as well.
  • Center St & 300 W rail crossings complete
  • Vineyard Connector arterial road established
  • Rail spur relocation for future Frontrunner station access
Housing and Transit Reinvestment Zone (HTRZ)
Vineyard was approved for an HTRZ designation which allows a portion of state sales tax revenue to be used in developing the area near the UTA Frontrunner station with housing that is accessible to transit.
Housing
A Variety of Housing Types
The Plan calls for a variety of housing types for a wide range of income levels. Thus far, over 3,800 residential housing units have been constructed and the city’s population has grown significantly (as of 2024).
  • 20+ neighborhoods built: Waters Edge, Lakefront, The Forge, Vineyard Shores
  • Zoning mix includes townhomes, single-family, and multi-family
Population Growth
As a result of significant growth in available housing in the city from the project, many residents have been able to make their homes in Vineyard. The city has been frequently recognized as one of the fastest growing in the US.
